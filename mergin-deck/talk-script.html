How it works

87:05 · 50 sec

Forms make your data consistent and reliable

Collect · 3 points · phone with a form

It starts with the form.

Everyone fills in the same fields, the same way. Value lists, dates, photos, relations: every QGIS field type works.

Questions only appear when they’re relevant. Date, surveyor and GPS accuracy fill in automatically. And constraints stop a bad value before it’s saved.

Errors get caught in the field, not back at the office.

129:45 · 55 sec

Every sync is a version

Share · “Latest changes” history

Every time someone syncs, Mergin Maps saves a new version.

You see who changed what, and when, down to a single attribute.

If two people edit the same layer offline, their changes merge, they don’t overwrite each other. That’s our open-source geodiff library at work.

And you can roll back to any earlier version.

For audited work, this is your evidence trail.

1310:40 · 55 sec

Same form every season. Comparable for years.

Long-term data · stacked maps 2022 to 2026

This is what all of that adds up to.

The same project and the same form, season after season. You’re not starting a new spreadsheet every year, and nobody renames a column halfway through.

So when you open 2022 next to 2026, the points are in the same places and the attributes mean the same thing. You can actually compare them.

Consistent, reliable data over the years. That’s what makes long-term monitoring work.

1411:35 · 1 min

Precision in, automation out

External GPS · APIs and database sync

Two more things for the people who need them.

Precision: a phone gives you a few meters. Plug in an external GPS receiver, from Emlid, Trimble, Bad Elf and others, and you get centimeter accuracy, stored with every feature.

Automation: there are Python and C++ clients, two-way sync with PostGIS, media sync to S3 or Google Drive, and OGC API access.

Everything you’ve seen can be automated.

1813:55 · 1 min

BGS: before and after

Before / After · Result: 2,000+ localities in year one

Before: their system, BGS·SIGMA, ran on ArcGIS since 2005. In March 2024, Esri retired that platform, so the whole system had to go. They used rugged Windows tablets: heavy, expensive, and in a town they draw attention. And they were locked to one vendor.

After: one QGIS project that works the same on tablets and phones. That, and clean multi-user merging, is why they picked Mergin Maps. The forms have constraints, so bad data can’t be saved. And the geologists use ordinary phones. Someone tapping on a phone just blends in.

Result: more than 2,000 localities mapped in the first year.

Old data and new data, side by side, and they agree. That’s what “reliable over the years” means.

2015:45 · 25 sec

Case study: EDI Environmental Dynamics

Field technician with a tablet

The second story is closer to home. EDI is an environmental consultancy with thirty years of fieldwork across northern and western Canada.

2116:10 · 55 sec

EDI: before and after

Before / After · Result: ~100 active projects

Before: crews used one app to navigate and another to fill in forms, and the data stayed stuck on each device. License costs were hard to predict. And when something broke, they waited on the vendor.

After: they found Mergin Maps on a QGIS thread on Reddit, tested it for a year, then rolled it out to about 180 staff. Because the code is open and every change is logged, they fix problems themselves.

Result: about 100 active projects, and 200 planned for next year.

All on the same open-source stack you’ve seen today.
